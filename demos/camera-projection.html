Pinhole camera: intrinsic and extrinsic parameters

A pinhole camera turns a 3D point into a pixel. Five numbers inside the camera stretch, shear and shift that picture. Six more place and aim the camera in the world. Drag the view on the left to look around. The sliders change the photograph on the right, and they are not moved for you.

Try this

  • Increase only fx. The tree stretches horizontally. The grey lines stay at the reset picture.
  • Set s away from zero. Vertical lines of the tree lean: that is shear.
  • Move u0. The whole picture shifts, and the red principal point moves with it.
  • Change yaw or tx. The tree moves because the camera moved, not because K changed.
How it works (technical details)

Principle

A scene point in the world is written as a 4-vector. The camera first expresses that point in its own axes, then applies the intrinsic matrix. Dividing by the third component removes the homogeneous scale and gives the pixel. Points with camera depth zcam ≤ 0 are behind the camera and are not drawn. Lens distortion is ignored.

Formulas

w [u,v,1]T =K [R∣t] [x,y,z,1]T
K= [ fxsu0 0fyv0 001 ]

Processing steps

  1. Build R from yaw, pitch and roll, then Xcam = R X + t.
  2. Apply K to Xcam. The third entry of the result is w = zcam.
  3. If zcam ≤ 0, discard the point. Otherwise the pixel is (u, v) = (first / w, second / w).

Reading the picture

The green lines are the tree at the current sliders. The grey lines are the same tree at the reset values of K and of [R | t]. A change in fx or fy scales the picture. A nonzero s shears it. u0 and v0 translate it, and the red cross is the principal point (u0, v0). Yaw, pitch, roll and t move the camera, so the grey and green drawings separate for a different reason.

Symbols and degrees of freedom

fx and fy are focal lengths in pixels. s is the skew. (u0, v0) is the principal point. Those five numbers are the intrinsic degrees of freedom. Yaw, pitch and roll, together with the three entries of t, are the six extrinsic degrees of freedom. The image here is 640 × 480 pixels. Dragging the left view does not change any of these eleven numbers.

Frames and the rotation used here

World axes: x to the right, y up, z toward the default viewer. Camera axes: x to the right, y down, z forward along the optical axis. R = Rz(roll) Rx(pitch) Ry(yaw) R0, with R0 = diag(1, −1, −1). At zero angles, camera x equals world x, camera y equals −world y, and camera z equals −world z, so the camera looks from positive world z toward the origin. t is the world origin written in the camera frame, not a position you add in world coordinates. The worked example uses the tip of the tree.

The count of eleven degrees of freedom, and the split into five intrinsic and six extrinsic, follows the lecture “Degrees of freedom” by James Hays, as used in the FAU course.
